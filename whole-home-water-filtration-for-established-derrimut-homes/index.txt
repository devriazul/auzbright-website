40:T2ba2,<p>Whole home water filtration is often associated with new builds or major renovations, but an established home may also be suitable for a retrofit.</p>
<p>The key question is not simply how old the property is. What matters more is whether the incoming water supply can be accessed, whether there is suitable space for the filtration system, and whether future servicing can be carried out without creating unnecessary disruption.</p>
<h2 id="can-whole-home-filtration-be-retrofitted-to-an-existing-home">Can whole home filtration be retrofitted to an existing home?</h2>
<p>Yes, whole home water filtration can often be retrofitted to an established property if the incoming mains water line is accessible and there is enough suitable space for the equipment and future servicing.</p>
<p>However, every property is different. Some homes may need straightforward plumbing changes, while others may require additional pipework or a different installation position.</p>
<p>A property assessment should therefore come before any promise that the system can be installed without building or landscaping changes.</p>
<h2 id="1-start-by-locating-where-the-mains-water-enters-the-propert">1. Start by locating where the mains water enters the property</h2>
<p>A whole home filtration system works by filtering water before it is distributed through the household plumbing.</p>
<p>For a retrofit, the installer first needs to identify where the incoming supply enters the property.</p>
<p>Depending on the home, this may be near:</p>
<ul>
<li>an external wall</li>
<li>a side passage</li>
<li>the water meter</li>
<li>a garage</li>
<li>a utility area</li>
<li>an existing plumbing service zone</li>
</ul>
<p>The important question is whether the main water line can be accessed at a practical point before it branches off to different parts of the home.</p>
<p>If the pipework is concealed or difficult to reach, additional plumbing work may be required.</p>
<h2 id="2-check-whether-there-is-suitable-installation-space">2. Check whether there is suitable installation space</h2>
<p>Finding the incoming water line is only part of the assessment.</p>
<p>The system also needs a practical location where it can be installed securely and accessed later.</p>
<p>The proposed position should allow for:</p>
<ul>
<li>the filtration equipment itself</li>
<li>safe plumbing connections</li>
<li>servicing access</li>
<li>cartridge replacement</li>
<li>access to the system bypass</li>
<li>protection from accidental damage</li>
<li>practical access for future maintenance</li>
</ul>
<p>An area that appears convenient today may become difficult to service once fencing, storage, landscaping or other equipment is installed around it.</p>
<p>This is why retrofit planning should consider long-term access, not only whether the system physically fits.</p>
<h2 id="3-will-walls-or-floors-need-to-be-opened">3. Will walls or floors need to be opened?</h2>
<p>Not necessarily.</p>
<p>If the incoming water line is exposed or easy to access, a filtration retrofit may be possible without opening finished internal walls or floors.</p>
<p>However, this cannot be guaranteed for every property.</p>
<p>Additional work may be required if:</p>
<ul><li>the mains line is concealed</li>
<li>the proposed filtration location is far from the existing pipework</li>
<li>the plumbing has been altered during previous renovations</li>
<li>there is limited external access</li>
<li>existing services occupy the preferred installation area</li>
<li>the water line needs to be rerouted</li>
</ul>
<p>The installer should explain the proposed plumbing route before work begins.</p>
<p>For an established Derrimut home, this is more useful than assuming the filtration system will fit wherever there happens to be empty space.</p>
<h2 id="4-how-does-the-three-stage-system-fit-into-an-existing-home">4. How does the three-stage system fit into an existing home?</h2>
<p>Auzbright’s Whole Home Water Filtration System uses three filtration stages.</p>
<h3><b>Stage 1 — Sediment Filtration</b></h3>
<p>The first stage is designed to reduce sediment and particles such as dirt, rust, sand and silt.</p>
<h3><b>Stage 2 — Mineral Particle Reduction</b></h3>
<p>The second stage is designed to reduce mineral particles and may help limit scale build-up in household plumbing, fittings and water-using equipment.</p>
<h3><b>Stage 3 — Chlorine, Taste and Odour Improvement</b></h3>
<p>The third stage helps improve chlorine-related taste and odour before water travels to connected household outlets.</p>
<p>In an established property, these stages are incorporated into the main water-supply route rather than requiring a separate filter at every tap.</p>
<p>The exact plumbing configuration still depends on the home.</p>
<h2 id="5-why-does-servicing-access-matter-during-a-retrofit">5. Why does servicing access matter during a retrofit?</h2>
<p>A water filtration system is not something that should be installed in a location that becomes inaccessible after the plumber leaves.</p>
<p>Cartridges require servicing, and maintenance needs can vary depending on household usage and incoming water conditions.</p>
<p>Before choosing the location, ask:</p>
<ul><li>Can the cartridges be accessed comfortably?</li>
<li>Is there space to remove and replace them?</li>
<li>Can the bypass be reached?</li>
<li>Will garden structures or storage block the system later?</li>
<li>Can a service technician reach the equipment without entering difficult areas of the home?</li>
</ul>
<p>A location that looks discreet may not always be the best servicing location.</p>
<h2 id="6-what-is-the-role-of-the-built-in-bypass">6. What is the role of the built-in bypass?</h2>
<p>Auzbright’s filtration system includes a built-in bypass.</p>
<p>The bypass provides a way for the filtration section to be isolated when appropriate, such as during certain servicing situations.</p>
<p>For a retrofit, the important issue is making sure the bypass remains accessible after installation.</p>
<p>Homeowners should also ask the installer to explain:</p>
<ul><li>where the bypass is located</li>
<li>when it may be used</li>
<li>whether the homeowner should operate it</li>
<li>what happens to the household water supply during servicing</li>
</ul>
<p>Detailed operating instructions should come from the supplied product and installation documentation.</p>
<h2 id="7-check-what-else-is-already-installed-near-the-water-entry-">7. Check what else is already installed near the water entry point</h2>
<p>Established homes may already have several services competing for the same space.</p>
<p>This can include:</p>
<ul><li>hot water equipment</li>
<li>gas services</li>
<li>electrical equipment</li>
<li>irrigation connections</li>
<li>rainwater systems</li>
<li>air-conditioning equipment</li>
<li>storage</li>
<li>fencing</li>
<li>landscaping</li>
</ul>
<p>The filtration system should not be positioned without considering these existing services.</p>
<p>If you are also planning a future heat pump hot water upgrade, for example, it may make sense to review both equipment locations before committing valuable service-area space.</p>
<h2 id="8-can-filtration-be-added-while-the-home-is-occupied">8. Can filtration be added while the home is occupied?</h2>
<p>In many cases, filtration work can be assessed for an occupied home, but the installation scope will depend on the property.</p>
<p>The household should ask:</p>
<ul><li>how long the water supply may need to be isolated</li>
<li>which areas installers need to access</li>
<li>whether outdoor access must be cleared</li>
<li>whether plumbing changes are required</li>
<li>whether any landscaping or stored items need to be moved</li>
<li>what testing or commissioning is completed afterwards</li>
</ul>
<p>Avoid assuming that every retrofit has the same installation time or level of disruption.</p>
<h2 id="9-whole-home-filtration-is-separate-from-veu">9. Whole home filtration is separate from VEU</h2>

<p>Auzbright also provides energy-efficiency services such as heat pump hot water and air conditioning, but this does not mean its filtration product automatically qualifies for government support.</p>
<p>The decision to install filtration should instead be based on:</p>
<ul><li>household filtration goals</li>
<li>property suitability</li>
<li>plumbing access</li>
<li>installation requirements</li>
<li>maintenance planning</li>
<li>a property-specific quote</li>
</ul>
<p>No VEU connection should be assumed unless an official program source confirms otherwise.</p>
