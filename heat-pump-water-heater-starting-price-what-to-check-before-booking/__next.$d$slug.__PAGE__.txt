<h2 id="direct-answer-what-should-homeowners-check-before-booking">Direct Answer: What should homeowners check before booking?</h2>
<p>Before booking a heat pump water heater, homeowners should check their existing hot water system, property suitability, household hot water demand, available installation space, eligibility, quote inclusions, product options and any extra site requirements. A “starting from $700” price should be treated as a starting point, not a guaranteed final price for every home.</p>
<h2 id="1-understand-what-starting-from-700-means">1. Understand what “starting from $700” means</h2>
<p>A starting price usually applies to selected situations where the property, product, installation and eligibility conditions match the offer. It may not apply to every home, every system size, every location or every installation type.</p>
<p>The final cost can be affected by:</p>
<ul><li>current hot water system type</li>
<li>state-based eligibility</li>
<li>approved product options</li>
<li>household hot water demand</li>
<li>installation location</li>
<li>plumbing and electrical requirements</li>
<li>access to the installation area</li>
<li>removal of the old system if required</li>
<li>any extra site works</li>
</ul>
<p>This is why an eligibility check and quote are important before making a decision.</p>
<h2 id="2-check-your-existing-hot-water-system-first">2. Check your existing hot water system first</h2>
<p>Your current hot water system can affect the upgrade pathway. A home with an old electric storage system may have different requirements from a home with gas hot water, solar-boosted hot water or a more complex setup.</p>
<p>Before contacting Auzbright, it helps to know:</p>
<ul><li>Is your current system electric, gas or solar boosted?</li>
<li>Is it storage or instantaneous?</li>
<li>How old is it?</li>
<li>Is it leaking, noisy or slow to recover?</li>
<li>Does it run out of hot water?</li>
<li>Is it located indoors, outdoors, in a garage or in a tight access area?</li>
</ul>
<p>These details help Auzbright understand whether a heat pump water heater may be suitable and what needs to be checked before booking.</p>
<h2 id="3-check-whether-your-home-is-suitable">3. Check whether your home is suitable</h2>
<p>Heat pump water heaters use heat from the surrounding air to help heat water. Because of this, installation location matters. The system may need suitable outdoor space, airflow, drainage, access and enough room for the tank and unit.</p>
<p>Property suitability can vary between houses, townhouses, apartments, rental properties and homes with limited side access. A simple installation at one property may be more complex at another.</p>
<p>A quick suitability check should review:</p>

</thead>

<tr><td>Access</td><td>Some installations may require electrical checks</td></tr>

<h2 id="4-do-not-choose-only-by-price">4. Do not choose only by price</h2>
<p>Price is important, but it should not be the only factor. A heat pump water heater should suit your household’s daily hot water use. A smaller household may have different needs from a family with multiple bathrooms, frequent laundry use and high morning or evening demand.</p>
<p>Before booking, ask:</p>
<ul><li>How many people live in the home?</li>
<li>How many bathrooms are used regularly?</li>
<li>When is hot water used most?</li>
<li>Does the current system keep up?</li>
<li>Is the suggested system suitable for household demand?</li>
<li>What product options are available?</li>
</ul>
<p>A cheaper starting point may not be the right fit if the system does not suit your household.</p>
<p>A commercial upgrade should be planned carefully so the business understands what will happen before, during and after installation.</p>
<h2 id="5-check-eligibility-before-relying-on-discounts">5. Check eligibility before relying on discounts</h2>
<p>Energy-efficiency upgrade support can vary by state and program. Victorian households may need to check Victorian Energy Upgrades requirements where relevant. NSW households may need to check NSW energy-saving pathways where applicable. Other states may have different rules or different availability.</p>
<p>Do not assume that every property qualifies or that every advertised price applies to every home. Eligibility can depend on the state, property, current system, product, installation and current program rules.</p>
<p>Auzbright can help Australian homeowners check eligibility before relying on a starting price or rebate claim.</p>
<h2 id="6-ask-what-is-included-in-the-quote">6. Ask what is included in the quote</h2>
<p>A proper quote should be clear about what is included and what is not. Before booking, ask whether the quote includes:</p>
<ul><li>supply of the heat pump water heater</li>
<li>standard installation</li>
<li>removal or disconnection of the old system if required</li>
<li>plumbing work</li>
<li>electrical work</li>
<li>certificates or paperwork</li>
<li>eligibility assessment</li>
<li>any additional site requirements</li>
<li>exclusions or possible extra costs</li>
</ul>
<p>This helps avoid confusion later. The goal is to understand the real quote for your home, not just the starting price.</p>
<p>Avoid making the decision only on a headline discount. The better question is whether the quote suits your business site, hot water demand and upgrade requirements.</p>
<h2 id="7-be-careful-with-savings-claims">7. Be careful with savings claims</h2>
<p>A heat pump water heater may help reduce hot water energy use compared with some older systems, but savings vary. Usage, system type, tariffs, installation, weather, maintenance and household demand can all affect the outcome.</p>
<p>Avoid relying on fixed savings claims without an assessment. A responsible provider should explain that results are different from home to home.</p>
