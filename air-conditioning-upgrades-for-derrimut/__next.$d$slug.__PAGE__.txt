<h2 id="direct-answer-what-should-homeowners-assess-before-switching">Direct Answer: What should homeowners assess before switching?</h2>
<p>Before replacing older gas heating with air conditioning, homeowners should identify the current heater type, decide whether they need room-by-room or whole-home comfort, review insulation and room usage, check electrical readiness, plan indoor and outdoor unit locations, understand decommissioning requirements and confirm current VEU eligibility.</p>
<p>Reverse-cycle air conditioning may suit many homes, but the system and installation should be selected for the individual property.</p>
<h2 id="1-identify-the-gas-heating-system-you-already-have">1. Identify the gas-heating system you already have</h2>
<p>The first step is to understand what will be replaced.</p>
<p>Your home may currently use:</p>
<ul>
<li>ducted gas heating</li>
<li>a non-ducted gas room heater</li>
<li>a wall furnace</li>
<li>several separate gas heaters</li>
<li>gas heating combined with older cooling units</li>
<li>a system that heats only part of the property</li>
</ul>
<p>The current setup affects which reverse-cycle options may be practical and what work may be required.</p>
<p>For example, replacing one room heater may lead to a different solution from replacing whole-home ducted gas heating. The quote should clearly identify the existing system and explain what will be removed, disconnected or retained.</p>
<h2 id="2-decide-whether-you-need-targeted-or-whole-home-comfort">2. Decide whether you need targeted or whole-home comfort</h2>
<p>Not every household needs the same air-conditioning arrangement.</p>
<p>A homeowner who mainly uses the living room and one bedroom may prefer targeted split systems. A larger household that regularly uses several rooms may consider a multi-head or ducted solution.</p>

<h2 id="3-review-which-rooms-actually-need-heating-and-cooling">3. Review which rooms actually need heating and cooling</h2>
<p>Older gas systems are often designed around a fixed whole-home or single-room approach. Before replacing the system, consider how the property is used now.</p>
<p>Ask:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which rooms are occupied every day?</li>
<li>Are bedrooms mainly used at night?</li>
<li>Does anyone work from home?</li>
<li>Are some areas rarely occupied?</li>
<li>Is the living area open plan?</li>
<li>Does the upstairs level behave differently from downstairs?</li>
<li>Are there rooms that become particularly hot in summer?</li>
<li>Will household usage change in the next few years?</li>
</ul>
<p>This helps prevent over-planning or installing a system that does not match daily routines.</p>
<h2 id="4-assess-insulation-draughts-and-window-exposure">4. Assess insulation, draughts and window exposure</h2>
<p>A new air conditioner should not be expected to solve every comfort problem on its own.</p>
<p>Poor insulation, large areas of glass, draughts, direct afternoon sun and open staircases can affect how a home heats and cools. These factors may influence system selection and room-by-room performance.</p>
<p>Before approving an upgrade, it may be useful to review:</p>
<ul>
<li>ceiling and wall insulation</li>
<li>gaps around doors and windows</li>
<li>window orientation</li>
<li>curtains or shading</li>
<li>room size and ceiling height</li>
<li>open-plan areas</li>
<li>air movement between levels</li>
</ul>
<p>The objective is to select a suitable system while also recognising features of the home that may affect comfort and energy use.</p>
<h2 id="5-check-electrical-readiness">5. Check electrical readiness</h2>
<p>Switching from gas heating to electric reverse-cycle air conditioning may change the home’s electrical requirements.</p>
<p>Depending on the selected system and existing property setup, the installer may need to review:</p>
<ul>
<li>switchboard condition</li>
<li>available electrical capacity</li>
<li>dedicated circuits</li>
<li>wiring routes</li>
<li>meter or supply considerations</li>
<li>the location of indoor and outdoor units</li>
<li>whether additional electrical work is required</li>
</ul>
<p>Not every property will need major electrical changes. However, electrical requirements should be checked before the final quote is accepted.</p>
<h2 id="6-plan-outdoor-unit-placement-carefully">6. Plan outdoor-unit placement carefully</h2>
<p>Reverse-cycle systems generally require outdoor equipment. Its placement should be considered early, especially for properties with limited side access or neighbouring homes nearby.</p>
<p>The assessment should consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>safe installer access</li>
<li>suitable airflow</li>
<li>space around the unit</li>
<li>distance from bedrooms and living areas</li>
<li>proximity to neighbours</li>
<li>visibility from the street</li>
<li>pipework routes</li>
<li>future servicing access</li>
<li>owners-corporation rules where relevant</li>
</ul>
<p>Product-specific installation requirements should be confirmed for the selected model rather than estimated from a general advertisement.</p>
<h2 id="7-understand-what-happens-to-the-gas-heater">7. Understand what happens to the gas heater</h2>
<p>Homeowners should ask what decommissioning or removal work forms part of the proposed upgrade.</p>
<p>The quote should explain:</p>
<ul>
<li>whether the old heater will be removed</li>
<li>whether it will be disconnected or left in place</li>
<li>what happens to existing ducts or vents</li>
<li>whether gas-related work is included</li>
<li>whether wall or floor openings need attention</li>
<li>whether certificates will be supplied</li>
<li>whether separate trades or costs may apply</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not assume that installing the new air conditioner automatically includes every part of removing the old gas-heating system.</p>
<h2 id="8-check-current-veu-eligibility">8. Check current VEU eligibility</h2>
<p>Eligible Victorian households may be able to access support for approved reverse-cycle air-conditioning upgrades under the Victorian Energy Upgrades program.</p>
<p>However, eligibility and the applicable upgrade pathway depend on current program requirements, the existing heating system, the proposed replacement, approved products and installation details.</p>
<p>Current requirements should be confirmed before proceeding. Homeowners should not assume:</p>
<ul>
<li>every gas-heated home receives the same support</li>
<li>every reverse-cycle model is eligible</li>
<li>the same discount applies to all properties</li>
<li>approval is automatic</li>
<li>an advertised amount will be the final out-of-pocket price</li>
</ul>
<p>Auzbright can help Derrimut homeowners understand available pathways and identify the information needed for an eligibility assessment.</p>
<h2 id="9-compare-the-full-quote">9. Compare the full quote</h2>
<p>The lowest headline price may not represent the same installation scope as another proposal.</p>
<p>Before accepting a quote, check whether it includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>the proposed air-conditioning system</li>
<li>indoor and outdoor units</li>
<li>standard installation</li>
<li>electrical work</li>
<li>pipework and drainage</li>
<li>brackets or mounting</li>
<li>removal or decommissioning of the gas heater</li>
<li>duct, vent or zone requirements</li>
<li>certificates and paperwork</li>
<li>VEU assessment where relevant</li>
<li>exclusions and possible additional costs</li>
</ul>
<p>A complete quote should also explain why the recommended system suits the property.</p>
<h2 id="10-consider-whether-other-gas-appliances-will-remain">10. Consider whether other gas appliances will remain</h2>
<p>Replacing gas heating does not necessarily mean the home will stop using gas entirely. The property may still have gas hot water, cooking or other appliances.</p>
<p>Before making broader decisions about the gas connection, confirm:</p>
<ul>
<li>which gas appliances remain</li>
<li>whether they will be replaced now or later</li>
<li>whether the household is planning staged electrification</li>
<li>whether supply charges remain relevant</li>
<li>whether solar or future electrical upgrades are planned</li>
</ul>
<p>This avoids treating one air-conditioning upgrade as though it automatically changes the whole property’s energy setup.</p>
