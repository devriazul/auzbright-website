Ducted vs Split System Air Conditioning: Which Upgrade Fits Your Home?

21 June 20266 min readBy the Auzbright team

Choosing between ducted and split system air conditioning is not only about cooling power. The better option depends on your home layout, how many rooms you want to heat or cool, when different areas are used, your installation conditions, and how much control you want over each space.

A split system air conditioner is usually used for one room or a specific zone, such as a bedroom, lounge room or home office. A ducted system is designed to distribute heating and cooling through multiple rooms using ducts and vents, often with zoning options.

If you are considering a more efficient heating and cooling system, Auzbright can help you understand your air conditioning upgrade options and eligibility before you decide.

Direct Answer: Is ducted or split system air conditioning better?

Neither ducted nor split system air conditioning is automatically better for every home. A split system may suit homeowners who want targeted heating and cooling for one or two rooms, while ducted air conditioning may suit homes that need whole-home comfort across several areas. The right choice depends on layout, usage, budget, installation access, zoning needs and long-term comfort goals.

1. What is a split system air conditioner?

A split system has an indoor unit and an outdoor unit. The indoor unit is usually installed on a wall inside the room, while the outdoor unit sits outside the home. Many split systems are reverse-cycle, meaning they can provide both heating and cooling.

Split systems are commonly used for:

  • bedrooms
  • living rooms
  • home offices
  • small apartments
  • single-room upgrades
  • targeted heating and cooling
  • replacing older room air conditioners

The main advantage is control. You can heat or cool the room you are using without needing to condition the whole home. This can be practical for households where only one or two spaces are used most of the time.

2. What is ducted air conditioning?

Ducted air conditioning uses a central unit connected to ducts that distribute air through vents in different rooms. Depending on the system design, it may include zones so different parts of the home can be controlled separately.

Ducted systems are often considered when homeowners want:

  • heating and cooling across multiple rooms
  • a cleaner look with fewer visible indoor units
  • comfort across larger homes
  • zoning between living areas and bedrooms
  • an integrated whole-home solution

Ducted air conditioning can be a good fit for some homes, but it may require more installation planning. Roof space, ceiling access, duct routes, insulation, zoning design and electrical requirements all matter.

4. Which option suits smaller homes or apartments?

For smaller homes, apartments, townhouses or units, a split system may often be the more practical option. If the main comfort issue is one bedroom, a living area or a home office, a targeted split system can make more sense than planning a whole-home ducted system.

A split system may also be easier to consider where ceiling access is limited or ductwork is not practical. However, apartment owners may need to check body corporate rules, outdoor unit placement and installation access before moving ahead.

5. Which option suits larger homes?

Larger homes may benefit from ducted air conditioning if the goal is to manage comfort across several rooms. Ducted systems can be useful when the family wants one integrated solution instead of multiple indoor wall units.

However, the system should be designed carefully. Zoning is important because it may not be efficient or practical to heat or cool every room at the same time when only some areas are being used.

For example, a family may want living areas controlled during the day and bedrooms controlled at night. A well-planned ducted setup can support this kind of use, but the design needs to match the home.

6. Think about how your household uses each room

Before choosing, ask how the home is used during a normal week.

Do you mainly use one living space and one bedroom? A split system may be enough. Do you have children using different bedrooms, a home office, and shared living zones? Ducted or multiple split systems may need to be compared.

Useful questions include:

  • Which rooms need heating and cooling most?
  • Are all rooms used every day?
  • Do people use different rooms at the same time?
  • Is comfort needed during the day, evening or overnight?
  • Are there rooms that rarely need air conditioning?
  • Would zoning help avoid conditioning unused spaces?

Auzbright can help homeowners work through these practical questions before choosing an upgrade.

7. Compare installation conditions, not just system type

A common mistake is choosing the system type before checking the property. Installation conditions can affect what is realistic and suitable.

For split systems, check:

  • indoor wall space
  • outdoor unit location
  • drainage
  • electrical access
  • distance between indoor and outdoor units
  • noise and airflow considerations

For ducted systems, check:

  • roof or ceiling space
  • duct pathways
  • zoning design
  • return air location
  • insulation
  • access for installation and servicing
  • whether the home layout supports ducted airflow

The right upgrade should suit the building, not just the brochure.

8. What about energy use and running costs?

Both ducted and split system air conditioning can be energy-efficient when the system is correctly selected, installed and used. Running costs can vary based on system size, star rating, temperature settings, insulation, weather, tariffs, zoning and household behaviour.

A split system may be efficient for heating or cooling one room because it targets a smaller area. A ducted system may suit whole-home comfort, especially when zoning is used properly. But if a ducted system regularly heats or cools unused rooms, energy use may increase.

This is why sizing, zoning and usage habits matter. Avoid any claim that one system will always save more for every household.

9. Where eligibility may come into the decision

In Victoria, some eligible households may be able to access support for approved heating and cooling upgrades through relevant energy-efficiency pathways. Eligibility can depend on the property, existing system, proposed product, installation conditions and current requirements.

This blog is a general comparison guide, so you should not assume that a ducted or split system automatically qualifies for support. If eligibility matters to your decision, ask Auzbright to check your situation before you approve the upgrade.

FAQs

Is ducted air conditioning better than split system air conditioning?

Ducted air conditioning may suit homes that need heating and cooling across several rooms. Split systems may suit targeted comfort in one or two spaces. The better choice depends on layout, usage, installation access and budget.

Is a split system cheaper to run than ducted air conditioning?

It can be cheaper to run a split system when you only need to heat or cool one room, but running costs vary. System size, efficiency rating, usage, zoning, insulation, tariffs and weather all affect the result.

Can ducted air conditioning be zoned?

Many ducted systems can be designed with zones, allowing different areas of the home to be controlled separately. Zoning can be useful when some rooms are used at different times of the day.

Can I install multiple split systems instead of ducted air conditioning?

In some homes, multiple split systems may be a practical alternative to ducted air conditioning. This depends on room layout, outdoor unit placement, electrical capacity, appearance preferences and budget.

Should I choose ducted or split system for a home office?

A split system may suit a home office if only that room needs regular heating and cooling during the day. If the whole home needs comfort at the same time, ducted options may be worth comparing.
