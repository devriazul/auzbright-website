Whole Home vs Under-Sink Water Filtration: Which Setup Suits a Western Melbourne Household?

25 August 20267 min readBy the Auzbright team

Choosing a water filtration system is not only about selecting a filter cartridge. It is also about deciding where you want water to be filtered and how many household outlets should receive it.

Auzbright is based in Derrimut and offers a three-stage Whole Home Water Filtration System for suitable properties. If you are comparing whole-home and under-sink filtration in western Melbourne, the first step is to decide whether you want filtration at one drinking-water point or throughout the property.

Direct Answer: Which filtration system should you choose?

Choose an under-sink system when your main goal is filtered water from one selected kitchen tap. Consider whole home filtration when you want water filtered before it reaches multiple connected household outlets.

Whole-home filtration involves a broader installation and servicing scope, while under-sink filtration is usually more targeted. Property suitability should be assessed before either option is selected.

1. Start with the water outlets you want to filter

The clearest difference between the two systems is coverage.

An under-sink filter is normally installed beneath the kitchen sink and connected to a dedicated or selected tap. Its purpose is generally to provide filtered water for drinking, cooking and filling containers.

Whole home filtration works earlier in the plumbing system. It filters water near the point where the supply enters the property, before that water travels to connected outlets.

This may include:

  • kitchen taps
  • bathroom basins
  • showers and baths
  • laundry taps
  • washing machines
  • dishwashers
  • hot water systems
  • other connected fixtures

The exact outlets served depend on the home’s plumbing layout and installation design.

This is a practical comparison rather than a health claim. Homeowners should not assume that either system will produce medical benefits or prevent illness.

3. How whole home filtration works

A whole home system is connected to the main incoming water line. Water passes through the filtration stages before moving through the property’s internal plumbing.

Auzbright’s system uses three stages:

Stage 1: Sediment filtration

The first stage is designed to reduce sediment and particles such as dirt, dust, rust, sand and silt.

Stage 2: Mineral-particle reduction

The second stage is designed to reduce mineral particles and may help limit scale build-up around taps, fixtures and water-using appliances.

Stage 3: Chlorine, taste and odour improvement

The third stage helps improve chlorine-related taste and odour before water reaches connected household outlets.

Results and maintenance needs vary according to the property, incoming water conditions and household usage.

4. How under-sink filtration works

An under-sink system is installed beneath a kitchen sink and connected to one selected outlet.

This can suit households that mainly want filtered water for:

  • drinking
  • cooking
  • preparing tea or coffee
  • filling bottles
  • washing fruit and vegetables

Because it focuses on one point, it does not normally filter the water used in showers, laundries or other rooms unless separate systems are installed.

The filter type, cartridge requirements and intended performance depend on the selected product. These details should be checked through the product documentation rather than assumed.

5. Compare installation requirements

Whole home filtration usually requires more installation planning because it connects to the property’s main water supply.

The assessment may need to review:

  • where the water line enters the property
  • available space for the filtration system
  • access to existing plumbing
  • safe servicing access
  • the location of the filtration cabinet
  • whether plumbing changes are required
  • whether a built-in bypass is included and how it operates

Under-sink installation is more localised, but it still needs suitable cupboard space, plumbing access and room for cartridge replacement.

For apartments, townhouses or properties with shared infrastructure, approval or common-property considerations may also need to be checked.

6. Consider servicing and cartridge access

Both filtration systems require maintenance. The difference is where and how that maintenance occurs.

With under-sink filtration, cartridges are normally accessed inside the kitchen cabinet. Storage items may need to be moved when servicing is required.

With whole home filtration, the system should be installed where the cartridges and bypass can be reached safely without obstructing daily use.

Maintenance requirements depend on:

  • incoming water quality
  • household consumption
  • filter design
  • cartridge type
  • manufacturer guidance
  • servicing conditions

Homeowners should avoid relying on a universal cartridge replacement schedule unless it is supported by the supplied product documentation.

7. Think about plumbing and appliance protection

One reason homeowners consider whole home filtration is that water is filtered before it travels through household plumbing.

By reducing selected sediment and mineral particles at the entry point, the system may help limit the amount of this material reaching:

  • tapware
  • shower fittings
  • washing machines
  • dishwashers
  • hot water systems
  • internal plumbing

An under-sink filter does not normally provide this broader coverage because it treats water after it has already travelled through most of the property.

These potential benefits should remain conditional. Whole home filtration should not be described as guaranteeing scale-free plumbing or preventing every appliance problem.

8. Could a household use both systems?

Some households may choose whole home filtration for water throughout the property and a separate under-sink system for a more specific drinking-water requirement.

A combined approach may be considered when the household wants:

  • broader sediment and mineral-particle reduction
  • filtration for bathrooms and laundries
  • an additional drinking-water stage at the kitchen
  • different filtration goals for different outlets

Whether both systems are necessary depends on household preferences, the whole-home product selected and the intended use of the under-sink filter.

A provider should explain the role of each system clearly so the household does not pay for duplicated functions it does not need.

9. Which option is better during a renovation?

Renovation can be a useful time to consider water filtration because plumbing access and equipment locations may be easier to plan before walls, cabinets and landscaping are completed.

For whole home filtration, early planning can help determine:

  • the main water entry point
  • cabinet location
  • pipe routing
  • servicing clearance
  • outdoor access
  • protection from weather
  • coordination with hot water equipment

For under-sink filtration, homeowners can plan cabinet space, tap placement and plumbing connections before the kitchen is finished.

Auzbright can discuss whole-home filtration during the planning stage so the proposed location can be reviewed before other renovation decisions are finalised.

10. Compare the full quote

Do not compare filtration systems by equipment price alone.

A quote should explain:

  • the filtration system supplied
  • number and purpose of filtration stages
  • installation work
  • plumbing changes
  • bypass arrangement where applicable
  • cartridge access
  • maintenance expectations
  • exclusions
  • possible site-specific costs
  • product support
  • any warranty terms supported by documentation

The homeowner should also understand which taps and appliances the system is expected to serve.

FAQs

Is whole home filtration better than an under-sink filter?

It depends on the household’s goal. Whole home filtration provides broader coverage, while an under-sink filter focuses on one selected kitchen outlet.

Does whole home filtration supply filtered water to showers and laundries?

It may supply filtered water to connected showers, bathroom taps and laundry outlets when installed on the main incoming water line. The exact coverage depends on the plumbing design.

Can I install both whole home and under-sink filtration?

Yes, some households may use both systems for different purposes. A provider should confirm whether the additional under-sink stage adds a useful function or duplicates existing filtration.

Does Auzbright offer whole home water filtration in western Melbourne?

Auzbright is based in Derrimut and offers a Whole Home Water Filtration System. Property suitability and service availability should be confirmed through an enquiry.

Is whole home water filtration covered under VEU?

No VEU connection should be assumed. Whole home water filtration is separate from eligible Victorian energy-efficiency upgrade pathways unless official confirmation is provided.
