Expanding a Solar System in Western Melbourne: What to Check First

21 August 20267 min readBy the Auzbright team

An existing rooftop solar system may no longer match the way your household uses electricity. You may have added air conditioning, started working from home, installed heat pump hot water, purchased an electric vehicle or simply noticed that more electricity is being used after sunset.

Auzbright is based in Derrimut and supports households considering solar and battery upgrades across western Melbourne. Before requesting extra panels or a battery, the first step should be an assessment of the system already installed.

Direct Answer: Can an existing solar system be expanded?

Some existing solar systems can be expanded, but not every system can accept additional panels or a battery without changes.

The answer depends on:

  • the age and condition of the current system
  • inverter capacity and compatibility
  • available roof space
  • panel type and electrical design
  • switchboard condition
  • electricity-network requirements
  • export limits
  • battery integration
  • household usage
  • future energy plans

In some cases, additional panels may be connected to the existing inverter. In others, the inverter may need to be replaced, a second inverter may be added, or the system may need to be redesigned.

1. Review how the current solar system is performing

Before adding equipment, check whether the existing system is working as expected.

Useful information may include:

  • original system size
  • installation date
  • panel and inverter brands
  • current electricity generation
  • monitoring history
  • fault or warning messages
  • electricity exported to the grid
  • daytime household use
  • recent electricity bills
  • installer paperwork and warranties

Monitoring data can help show whether the system is producing normally and whether excess solar is regularly being exported.

If the system is underperforming, the cause should be investigated before more panels are added.

2. Check the age and condition of the existing equipment

Older panels and inverters may still operate, but their condition should be reviewed before they become part of an expanded system.

Ask:

  • Is the inverter still reliable?
  • Are replacement parts available?
  • Are the panels showing visible damage?
  • Has the system had repeated faults?
  • Is the original installer documentation available?
  • Does the existing equipment meet current requirements?
  • Would combining old and new equipment affect design or support?

Expansion should not be used to hide an existing performance or safety problem.

3. Check whether more panels will fit on the roof

Available roof area is only one part of the panel decision.

An installer may also need to review:

  • roof orientation
  • shading
  • roof condition
  • panel layout
  • existing panel placement
  • access paths
  • roof penetrations
  • vents, skylights and antennas
  • structural suitability
  • future renovation plans

Roof Question

Why It Matters

Is suitable space available?

Additional panels need practical, usable roof area.

Is the area shaded?

Shade may affect output from the added panels.

Are old and new panels compatible?

Electrical characteristics and system design need checking.

Is the roof in good condition?

Repairs may be easier before new panels are installed.

Will future work affect the layout?

Renovations may require panels to be moved later.

Do not assume that every unused roof section is suitable for solar.

4. Can the existing inverter accept more panels?

The inverter is one of the most important parts of an expansion assessment. It converts electricity produced by the panels into power that can be used by the household or exported to the grid.

The installer should check:

  • inverter size
  • available input capacity
  • number of inputs
  • voltage and current limits
  • compatibility with proposed panels
  • condition and remaining useful life
  • network and export requirements
  • whether oversizing is permitted within the proposed design

Adding panels beyond what the inverter or system design can safely support is not appropriate.

Possible outcomes may include:

  1. keeping the existing inverter and adding a suitable number of panels
  2. replacing the existing inverter with a larger or hybrid model
  3. adding a second solar inverter
  4. redesigning the system as part of a broader upgrade

The correct option depends on the property and equipment.

5. Will network approval need to be updated?

Grid-connected solar systems operate under electricity-network connection requirements. Expanding the panel array, changing an inverter or adding battery equipment may require a new or updated connection application.

The process may review:

  • total inverter capacity
  • export limits
  • inverter settings
  • network conditions
  • battery configuration
  • metering
  • whether flexible exports apply
  • approval before commissioning

A homeowner should not assume that an older approval automatically covers a larger or redesigned system.

The solar provider should explain who handles the application and whether approval is required before installation.

6. Is the switchboard ready?

An expanded solar or battery system may require a review of the switchboard, meter and electrical protection.

Depending on the property, the installer may check:

  • available switchboard space
  • condition of existing equipment
  • circuit protection
  • isolation requirements
  • meter configuration
  • wiring
  • system labelling
  • battery connection requirements
  • whether electrical upgrades are necessary

Not every home will need switchboard work, but it should be assessed before the final quote is confirmed.

7. Can a battery be added to the existing solar system?

A battery may be added to many existing solar systems, but compatibility should be confirmed.

The system may use:

  • a hybrid inverter that manages solar and battery operation
  • a separate battery inverter
  • an AC-coupled battery arrangement
  • a redesigned solar-and-battery configuration

The installer should review the existing inverter, battery product, electrical layout and household usage before recommending an approach.

Ask:

  • Is the current inverter battery-ready?
  • Will it need to be replaced?
  • Does the battery include its own inverter?
  • Is the battery compatible with the existing system?
  • Where will it be installed?
  • Is backup included?
  • Will the battery support selected circuits or the whole home?
  • Is extra switchboard work required?

8. Do not assume every battery provides blackout backup

A battery does not automatically keep the home powered during an outage.

Backup capability depends on:

  • battery model
  • inverter configuration
  • wiring
  • backup hardware
  • selected circuits
  • available stored energy
  • system settings
  • household load during the outage

If backup is important, it should be included in the design and quote. The provider should explain which circuits may operate and what limitations apply.

No fixed backup duration should be assumed because it depends on battery charge, appliance demand and system design.

9. Review why you want to expand the system

The proposed upgrade should be connected to a clear household goal.

Common reasons include:

  • higher daytime electricity use
  • adding heat pump hot water
  • installing reverse-cycle air conditioning
  • charging an electric vehicle
  • working from home
  • reducing daytime grid use
  • storing excess solar for evening use
  • preparing for future electrification
  • replacing ageing equipment

The right solution may be more panels, a battery, an inverter replacement, load shifting or a combination of changes.

A larger system is not automatically the most suitable option.

10. Compare the complete quote

A solar-expansion quote should explain the relationship between the existing system and the proposed work.

Check whether it includes:

  • system inspection
  • proposed panel quantity and layout
  • inverter assessment
  • new inverter where required
  • battery and integration equipment
  • switchboard work
  • network application
  • metering requirements
  • monitoring setup
  • installation
  • product documentation
  • warranties
  • exclusions
  • removal or replacement of old equipment

The quote should clearly state which existing components will remain and which will be replaced.

FAQs

Can I add more panels to my existing solar system?

Possibly. The installer must check inverter capacity, roof space, panel compatibility, network requirements and the condition of the existing system.

Will I need a new inverter?

Not always. Some systems may accept additional panels, while others may need a larger inverter, a hybrid inverter or a second inverter.

Can I add a battery to an older solar system?

A battery may be added to some older systems, but the inverter and electrical configuration must be assessed for compatibility.

Will a battery power my home during a blackout?

Only if the system is designed and configured for backup. The quote should explain whether backup is included and which circuits it may support.

Does Auzbright help with existing solar systems in western Melbourne?

Auzbright is based in Derrimut and can help households review existing solar, inverter and battery upgrade options. Service availability and the property requirements should be confirmed through an enquiry.
